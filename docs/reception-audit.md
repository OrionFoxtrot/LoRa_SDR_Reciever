# DeSCENT LoRa reception audit

14 November 2026. This audit covers the Windows RTL2832U/R820T receiver at 1 MS/s. The hardware reference is a 915 MHz T-Beam fitted with an SX1276 and configured for raw LoRa. Their relative sensitivity at calibrated RF input levels still needs a side-by-side measurement. [LILYGO T-Beam hardware](https://wiki.lilygo.cc/products/t-beam-series/t-beam/) · [Semtech SX1276 datasheet, Rev. 7](https://cdn.sparkfun.com/assets/7/7/3/2/2/SX1276_Datasheet.pdf)

## Weak packets can disappear before the CRC check

At SF11 and −17.5 dB nominal in-band SNR, the receiver recovered **31 of 32** synthetic packets with a +1,464.84 Hz carrier offset and **0 of 32** at +1,495.36 Hz. Both offsets are well inside the configured search range. The 30.52 Hz difference moves the signal by half an SF11 FFT bin. Each pair used the same independently generated EPFL waveform, noise realization, transmitted phase and arrival time. All 32 half-bin cases failed to sync. Payload FEC retries never saw them. The detector uses the strongest integer FFT bin, fixed peak-to-mean gates, and a hard check on both sync symbols. Instrumenting those gates individually would identify which rejects each candidate. ## Fleet reception and unequal powers

One SX1276 listens on one carrier and SF at a time. The SDR can monitor four carriers at SF10–12 continuously. A paced 1 MS/s synthetic stream through the live reader recovered 48/48 packets across all 12 combinations. A fresh replay of physical bench IQ recovered seven SF11 packets from CSID 249 and three SF12 packets from CSID 250 on the same carrier, including overlapping transmissions. The separate zero-guard SF10 recording recovered counters 0–3 from each of two radios. All previously valid payloads and start samples matched their original journals. [12-branch loopback](../validation/monitor_profiles/four_carrier_loopback/run/20261114_155429_23eomuhe/score.json) · [Physical IQ replay results](../validation/reception_audit/20261114_195403_768fcbzv/recorded.json)

| Synthetic input, scored after decoding | Primary | Primary plus offline SIC |
|---|---:|---:|
| SF11/SF12 overlapping on one carrier | 35/44 packets | 44/44 packets |
| Random-phase fleet, two transmitting carriers | 188/300 packets | 267/300 packets |
| Unique ChipSats in the three complete fleet periods | 67, 60, 61 | 94, 86, 87 |
| Periods reaching 80 ChipSats | 0/3 | 3/3 |

At power differences of 24 and 30 dB in either direction, primary missed every weaker overlapping packet in this sweep. All 24 isolated controls passed. Each signed power difference had only two overlap trials. The fleet and overlap scores each had zero false and duplicate valid matches. Offline successive interference cancellation (SIC) subtracts fitted waveforms from qualifying decoded packets, then searches the residual once. An overlap with no initial donor gets no subtraction from those packets. These results describe this repeatable IQ, including its selected offsets and noise. A single SX1276 also has a capture effect during collisions. Its weak-packet recovery curve against these radios is unmeasured. ## Other ways reception can look healthier or worse than it is

- **The CRC failure count omits acquisition losses.** The fleet primary pass logged 224 frames, including 36 failing CRC. TX truth showed 112 missed transmissions. Thirty sync candidates failed the header check. Some candidates can be false acquisitions, so stage counts alone still cannot supply a transmit denominator. [Primary run](../validation/reception_audit/20261114_195403_768fcbzv/primary/20261114_195424_j9m577k0/summary.json) · [Scored transmissions](../validation/reception_audit/20261114_195403_768fcbzv/primary/20261114_195424_j9m577k0/score.json)
- **Preamble length can change the reported period.** With a 16-symbol transmitted preamble and the default setting of eight, all three strong packets decoded, but their reported starts were late by 65.536, 131.072 and 262.144 ms at SF10, SF11 and SF12. Configuring 16 restored the correct starts. A packet near a period boundary can be counted in the wrong period and its SIC reconstruction can be misaligned. [Timestamp probe](../validation/reception_audit/20261114_195403_768fcbzv/probes.json) · [Start calculation](../receiver.py)
- **Generic LoRa scoring can reject a correct reception.** An unrelated 55-byte frame in the saved bench fixture matched its transmitted bytes and passed PHY CRC. Generic LoRa displayed it. The usual scorer returned 0/1 because the DeSCENT CRC failed, and the frame was ineligible as a SIC donor. A generic PHY comparison needs its own scoring rule. Fleet trust should still require both CRCs. [Reproduction](../validation/reception_audit/20261114_195403_768fcbzv/generic_scoring/result.json) · [Scorer](../reports.py) · [SIC donor selection](../sic.py)
- **Capture has a startup window and a host dependency.** Auto Set consumes samples while measuring and changing gain. Recording and decoding start after tuning settles. A local DSP queue overflow discards live decoder blocks, even when recording retained those bytes. A socket interruption can end a run, and stock rtl_tcp has no upstream sample sequence counter. A standalone T-Beam can run its modem independently of the laptop when its firmware is already in receive mode. [Capture lifecycle](../run.py) · [Socket and queue](../sources.py)

## Features to build first

1. **Fractional-bin preamble acquisition.** Try a second half-bin hypothesis and accumulate evidence across consecutive chirps. Keep multiple plausible peaks when packets overlap. Apply the sync-word and explicit-header checks to the shortlist. Use the paired SF11 case as the regression test, then measure false packets per hour on quiet and noisy RF and backlog at 12 branches. Soft-header retries are a separate improvement for packets that actually reach the header.
2. **A bench comparison with calibrated RF levels.** Feed one known packet source through a splitter to the SDR and an SX1276 board. Measure the loss of each path and swap the splitter outputs. Match carrier, SF, BW, sync, header, CR, CRC, preamble, IQ polarity and low-data-rate optimization. Step the input in 1 dB increments near the sensitivity limit and record both receivers against unique TX counters. Use at least 300 packets at points used to estimate 1% PER, with confidence intervals. Repeat with a controlled blocker and with the equal-power SF11/SF12 pair, using a second T-Beam for the second SF. Log both-heard, SDR-only, T-Beam-only and neither-heard transmissions.
3. **Interference diagnostics at capture time.** Add a waterfall covering the capture band, per-carrier noise estimates, and a short IQ buffer that saves samples before and after ADC clipping or a rejected header. Show syncs, header rejects, PHY CRC passes and trusted packets per branch. Use a controlled attenuator or an RF preselector ahead of the ADC when testing blocker overload. A preselector only helps when it can attenuate the interferer relative to the wanted band.
4. **A field acquisition profile.** Expose the tested frequency-search width, retain the existing PPM correction, and estimate actual preamble length or flag uncertain start times. Exercise the wider search with all four carriers and overlapping traffic before promoting it to a default. Add an optional multi-candidate or iterative offline SIC pass for overlaps that still have an identifiable donor.
5. **Separate generic PHY accounting and recoverable host interruptions.** Give generic comparisons a PHY-valid, byte-exact score alongside the strict DeSCENT score. For unattended use, support reconnecting the SDR as a new timestamped capture segment with a visible unknown gap.

The current quick suite passed all 41 tests, the independent EPFL fixture scored 5/5, and the nine-branch simultaneous fixture scored 9/9. The additional audit probes used independent EPFL transmit waveforms for noise, offset and preamble tests. The 300-transmission and near/far fixtures used the existing synthetic IQ.
